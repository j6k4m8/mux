A high-efficiency inbox that is not super ugly.

Mux is a free, open-source, not-hideous mail client. Its key features are (1) not bad, and (2) not ugly.

I made mux because all of the current options are kinda sucky.

Webmail

Ads. Tracking. Vendor lock-in. No offline access.

Aging open-source clients

Archaic looks, atrocious UX. Did my best to love them but alas

Premium clients

Subscription fees to read your own email: literally lol, lmao even

Mux is the fourth option. FOSS, beautiful, no sucky subscription fees.

Mux doesn't suck, and moreover, it's actually kinda nice:

Local-first

Reading, searching, and sorting never hit the network, so they can operate fast and offline. And you can queue messages for delivery when you reconnect.

Every inbox in one place

Multi-account with a unified inbox, and per-account accent colors so you always know which account you are in.

Bring your own AI

If you want a model in your mail, you can BYO, and Mux will (optionally) cap its authority (i.e., read but no send). If you don't, there is no forced AI in Mux.

Keyboard shortcuts, keyboard shortcuts!!

j/k to move, Enter to open, ⌘F to search, ⌘P for the palette. Et cetera, and so on. (All customizable)

Mux on macOS (with dumb AI slop threads but you get the idea)

The rest of it

  • Threading with a chat-style thread view. Quoted replies and signatures fold away, which makes inbox zero a lot less work.
  • Remote images loaded only with permission. No tracking by default; enable images and entire domains if you like ’em!
  • Sophisticated search. Boolean operators, field filters, and natural-language dates: from:jordan AND (since:1y OR is:starred).
  • Snooze, undo-send, and an outbox queue. Push a thread to later, take back a send, and let queued actions retry with backoff.

Why is Mux a Tauri2 app?

I tried writing Mux in a native framework but ultimately, email is HTML and needs an HTML renderer. So I could either ship a flutter/etc app and a web renderer, or just a web renderer. So here we are.
